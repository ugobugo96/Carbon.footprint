You may be wondering what a carbon footprint is.

A carbon footprint is an estimate of all greenhouse gases in our atmosphere,
including methane, nitrous oxide, and fluorinated gases.

Let's go into a more detailed explanation!

You may also have heard of a carbon footprint being called larger or smaller.
This is because different activities can release different amounts of greenhouse gases.
For example, driving a car releases more greenhouse gases than walking or riding a bike.
It can also be determined by your diet because if you usually eat lots of meat it makes
it a larger, because the amount of food, land size and resources also are taken into account
when calculating your carbon footprint. Where as a plant diet has a smaller carbon output
because it takes less resources to grow foods such as vegetables, fruits, and grains.

How do you, as an individual, affect your own carbon footprint?

Your carbon footprint is determined by the activities you do in your daily life.
For example, if you drive a car to school or work every day, that will increase your
carbon footprint. If you use public transportation, walk, or ride a bike instead, that will decrease
your carbon footprint. The same goes for the food you eat and the products you buy.
If you buy products that are made with sustainable materials and have a smaller
environmental impact, that will decrease your carbon footprint. If you buy products that are
made with unsustainable materials and have a larger environmental impact, that will
increase your carbon footprint.
